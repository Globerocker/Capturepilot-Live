# CapturePilot Objection Bank

> **Purpose**: Every objection a federal-contracting buyer will throw — with Hormozi-style reframes, subtext reads, wrong-response autopsies, control questions, and downsell paths. This is a live sales tool, not a FAQ doc. Use it before calls, not during them.

---

## How to use this bank

Read the subtext first. The objection they SAY is rarely the objection they MEAN. Once you know what's actually going on, the reframe picks itself.

Rule of thumb: **never defend the price, never argue the merit. Anchor against the cost of inaction.** One federal contract at $150K pays for five years of Pro. Every "I'll think about it" is a month of missed opportunities scored 87% match while they're still searching SAM.gov by hand.

## THE 20 OBJECTIONS

### OBJECTION 1 — PRICE
**"This is too expensive."**

---

**What they actually mean**

"I don't yet see why this is worth more than what I'm paying for inaction." They haven't done the math. They're comparing $89/mo to zero, not to $0 revenue from opportunities they missed, or 10 hours/week of BD work they're doing manually and getting wrong.

---

**The WRONG response**

> "Well, we do have a lighter plan at $39/mo…"

You just trained them that price is negotiable. You've also signaled that you agree it's expensive — you just confirmed the objection. Now they're waiting for you to offer Free.

---

**The Hormozi reframe**

"Expensive compared to what? You're SAM.gov-registered, you've got your UEI, you've got certifications the government specifically wants to buy from. The pipeline is there. The question is: are you finding it before your competitors do?

CapturePilot runs every single day and scores 40,000+ federal opportunities against your NAICS codes, certifications, and target states. You see HOT/WARM/COLD — not a list of 2,000 results to sort through.

$89 a month is $2.97 a day. One federal contract in your sweet spot — say $180K janitorial services at a VA medical center — covers 170 years of CapturePilot at Pro pricing. The math isn't tight. The math is embarrassing."

---

**Follow-up question to take control**

"Can I ask — what's your average contract value when you win federal work?"

*(Let them answer. Then do the math with them out loud. If they say $200K, say: "So you need to win one contract every 189 years to break even on the tool. What's the real hesitation?")*

---

**Downsell path**

If the reframe doesn't land: "We do have a 14-day trial at full Pro access — no charge. You get 14 days to see what we find for you. If you run the trial and it surfaces zero opportunities worth pursuing, don't upgrade. But I've never seen that happen for a SAM-registered company with active certifications."

### OBJECTION 2 — PRICE
**"I can just do this myself for free on SAM.gov."**

---

**What they actually mean**

"I've been doing this for a while and I don't understand why I need a tool for something I already kind of do." They've also probably tried SAM.gov and know it's terrible — but they've anchored on the word "free."

---

**The WRONG response**

> "SAM.gov is really hard to use though, our interface is much better—"

Now you're a UI upgrade. You're $89/month for a better-looking spreadsheet. That's not the sale.

---

**The Hormozi reframe**

"You absolutely can. SAM.gov is free, publicly available, and we don't have any data you couldn't technically access yourself.

Here's the difference: SAM.gov will show you 2,200 new postings today with no ranking, no scoring against your profile, no NAICS match weight, no set-aside filter that actually works, no deadline urgency signal, no recompete radar for incumbents you should be replacing, and no SLED results at all — because state and local governments don't post on SAM.gov.

So yes, free. Completely free. How many hours a week are you currently spending on that process?"

*(Pause. Let them answer.)*

"And when you find a Sources Sought posted six weeks ago that closes in three days — is that still useful to bid on?"

---

**Follow-up question to take control**

"What does your current BD process look like on a typical week? Walk me through it."

*(Their answer will reveal every inefficiency you can fix. Map your features to their current pain.)*

---

**Downsell path**

Free tier exists — but don't lead with it. If they insist on free: "Start on the free tier, run it alongside your manual process for two weeks, compare the HOT matches you find versus what you would have found on SAM.gov. If it's a wash, walk away."

### OBJECTION 3 — PRICE
**"Other guys charge less. GovWin is [price], Bloomberg Government is [price]."**

---

**What they actually mean**

"I've shopped this category before, or I've heard of competitors." Subtext is often: "I want to feel like I'm getting a deal" or "I'm a sophisticated buyer and I comparison-shop."

---

**The WRONG response**

> "Well, GovWin starts at $8,000 a year and Bloomberg Government is $12,000 a year, so actually we're cheaper—"

Competing on price against enterprise tools while you're a $89/mo product is a trap. You win the comparison but you look like a downmarket alt.

---

**The Hormozi reframe**

"GovWin IQ and Bloomberg Government are built for capture managers at prime contractors — the BAE Systems BD team, the Booz Allen pipeline shop. They're excellent tools. They're also $6,000 to $15,000 a year, they require onboarding, a dedicated user, and they assume you have a BD department.

You're a five-person company trying to grow from $2M to $5M in federal revenue. You don't need a cockpit. You need a radar.

CapturePilot is purpose-built for small contractors — sub-100-employee firms with SBA certifications that want a daily match feed they can act on without hiring a capture manager. It's not cheaper. It's calibrated for a different buyer.

Who at your company would be the primary GovWin user?"

*(They'll usually laugh. The point lands.)*

---

**Follow-up question to take control**

"Have you ever used GovWin or Bloomberg before? What made you not stick with it?"

---

**Downsell path**

If they keep pushing on price: offer annual Pro at $854/yr — frames it as $71/mo and saves them $214 vs monthly. Real money, commits them to the year.

### OBJECTION 4 — TIME
**"The timing isn't right. We're really busy right now."**

---

**What they actually mean**

"I'm not in pain enough right now to change anything." When they're busy, it usually means they have current work — and current work means they've mentally de-prioritized pipeline. The danger: when their current contract ends, they'll be scrambling with no pipeline built.

---

**The WRONG response**

> "No problem, let me follow up in a couple months when things calm down."

You've just been scheduled to be forgotten. "A couple months" is a polite brush-off. You have zero leverage when you call back.

---

**The Hormozi reframe**

"Busy is actually the best time to build a pipeline — not the worst. Here's why: CapturePilot surfaces Sources Sought and pre-solicitation notices 6 to 18 months before a contract hits. The work you win in Q3 next year is being positioned right now.

If you wait until your current contract ends to start looking, you're responding to solicitations instead of shaping them. That's the difference between a 15% win rate and a 40% win rate.

The tool takes 15 minutes to set up. After that it runs in the background every day. Busy is exactly when it makes the most sense."

---

**Follow-up question to take control**

"When does your current contract period end? When are you going to need new revenue?"

*(Connect the urgency to their pipeline gap, not to your close timeline.)*

---

**Downsell path**

"Start the 14-day trial now, let it run in the background. You don't have to act on any of the matches this week. Just watch what it finds. Two weeks from now tell me if it's surfacing work worth bidding on."

### OBJECTION 5 — TIME
**"It's Q4. We're slammed. Call me in January."**

---

**What they actually mean**

"I'm not willing to add a decision to my plate right now." This is a stall. It may also be a real capacity constraint. Either way, January is the worst month for federal contracting — new fiscal year budgets aren't released, agencies are slow, and the pipeline looks thin.

---

**The WRONG response**

> "Totally understand — I'll put a note to follow up January 3rd."

You've been ghosted with a date attached. The call back will be: "Hey, just following up like you asked!" and they'll have forgotten the conversation entirely.

---

**The Hormozi reframe**

"I hear you — but I want to flag something: October 1st is the start of the federal fiscal year. That's when agency budgets reset. The Sources Sought and pre-solicitation activity that runs September through November is the early pipeline for Q1 and Q2 awards.

If you wait until January, you've missed the window to respond to market research and build CO relationships before solicitations drop. January looks quiet because people didn't build pipeline in October.

I'm not asking you to do anything this week. I'm asking you to let the tool run in the background for 14 days so you have data in hand when you do sit down to plan your Q1 BD."

---

**Follow-up question to take control**

"What does your federal pipeline look like for Q1 and Q2 right now? Anything in proposal stage?"

---

**Downsell path**

If they genuinely cannot commit: "Let me send you a Quick Company Check on your UEI — takes 30 seconds, no login. You'll see exactly what your federal footprint looks like and what opportunities you're currently a match for. That way when you do call me in January, you're not starting cold."

### OBJECTION 6 — TIME
**"I need to check with my partner / wife / business partner first."**

---

**What they actually mean**

Could be real (two-owner partnership where both sign off on expenses). Could be a soft no with a human shield. Could mean: "I like it but I'm not the decision-maker and I'm embarrassed to say so." Read the energy.

---

**The WRONG response**

> "Of course! Here's a one-pager you can share with them."

You've handed control to someone who was never in the room, has no context, and is now reviewing a cold PDF. 90% of these die.

---

**The Hormozi reframe**

"Absolutely — and I want to make sure they have the right context when you bring it to them. Can I ask: what's the main thing they'd want to know? Is it the ROI case, the risk side, the time commitment, or something else?"

*(Get the real objection out of the proxy.)*

"Here's what I'd recommend: take 10 minutes right now, set up the trial with your profile — NAICS codes, certifications, target states — and let it run until Tuesday. When you talk to your partner, you'll have actual data: here are the 12 HOT matches the tool found for us this week. That's a much stronger conversation than forwarding a PDF."

---

**Follow-up question to take control**

"If your partner is on board, what would the decision timeline look like? Are we talking days or weeks?"

*(If the answer is "I don't know" — they're the decision-maker and they're stalling.)*

---

**Downsell path**

Offer to run the demo/trial simultaneously: "Start the free trial now — if your partner says no after two weeks, you cancel, nothing charged. The downside is zero."

### OBJECTION 8 — TRUST
**"How do I know this actually works?"**

---

**What they actually mean**

"I've been burned by software that promised a pipeline and delivered a prettier list of the same junk." They're skeptical of AI and automation claims in GovCon specifically — and they're right to be, because a lot of those claims are garbage.

---

**The WRONG response**

> "Our algorithm uses AI to match your profile to the best opportunities—"

"AI" has become a trust-destroyer in B2B SaaS. As soon as you say AI, they're thinking chatbot hallucinations and marketing copy. Stop saying AI first.

---

**The Hormozi reframe**

"I'll be direct with you: we don't promise you'll win contracts. No software can do that. What we promise is that you'll stop missing the right ones.

The matching is deterministic — it's scoring your NAICS codes, set-aside certifications, target states, and contract size against 40,000+ active federal opportunities every day. That's not magic, that's math. The HOT/WARM/COLD ranking is rule-based, not a black box.

What I'd suggest: run the trial, and on day three, look at every HOT match. Check three of them on SAM.gov manually. Tell me if those are real opportunities you'd otherwise want to bid on. If the answer is no — cancel. If the answer is yes, you have your proof."

---

**Follow-up question to take control**

"What would a 'working' tool look like for you? If it surfaced five legitimate solicitations per week in your NAICS codes, would that be enough to justify $89?"

*(They almost always say yes. You've now set the bar and you can go prove it in the trial.)*

---

**Downsell path**

Offer a live demo of the Quick Checker on their actual company URL. Show don't tell.

### OBJECTION 9 — TRUST
**"Can I talk to one of your existing customers?"**

---

**What they actually mean**

"I want third-party validation because I don't fully trust your word yet." This is actually a positive buying signal. Skeptics who ask for references are often close to a decision.

---

**The WRONG response**

> "Sure, let me connect you with a few — I'll send some names next week."

If "next week" is your answer, you've lost momentum. This lives and dies on how fast you respond.

---

**The Hormozi reframe**

"Absolutely. Let me ask you this first though: what specifically would you want to know from them? Because the answers you're looking for might be faster to get another way — and then I can get you a reference conversation to confirm.

If it's 'does the matching actually work,' I can show you that right now with a trial. If it's 'are these real people who won contracts because of CapturePilot,' that's a different question — and yes, I can connect you.

What's the core thing you're trying to answer?"

*(Get the specific concern. Answer it directly. Then offer the reference conversation as a closer, not as a replacement for your pitch.)*

---

**Follow-up question to take control**

"If a customer told you 'it surfaced three solicitations I wouldn't have found otherwise and I won one of them' — is that enough for you to move forward?"

---

**Downsell path**

Build a references page or a Slack community. If you don't have one, the trial IS the reference — let them be their own proof.

### OBJECTION 19 — URGENCY
**"Send me some info. I'll review it and get back to you."**

---

**What they actually mean**

"I'm too polite to say no, and I want to get off this call." The "info" will sit in their inbox for three days and then get filed away. In GovCon, where BD cycles are long and buyers are busy, "send me info" is the professional brush-off.

---

**The WRONG response**

> "Sure! I'll send over our deck and case studies."

You've just agreed to become a PDF sitting next to 400 other PDFs.

---

**The Hormozi reframe**

"Happy to — but I want to make sure I send you the right thing. The deck we have is pretty generic. What would actually be useful is showing you what CapturePilot finds for your specific company — because that's what you'd actually be buying.

The Quick Company Check runs on your company's UEI or website. It's free, takes about 60 seconds, and you'll see the actual opportunity matches for your profile. I can either run it with you right now, or send you the link and you can run it yourself.

That's more useful than a PDF — it's your data."

---

**Follow-up question to take control**

"What's your UEI? I'll pull it up right now."

*(Convert "send me info" into a live demo. The link to the Quick Checker becomes the info — and it's interactive, personalized, and closes the gap.)*

---

**Downsell path**

Send the Quick Checker URL with a one-line note: "This is the tool — run it on your UEI and tell me what you see." Low friction, no PDF, opens the follow-up conversation with their own data.

### OBJECTION 20 — PROOF
**"What if it doesn't work? Is there a money-back guarantee?"**

---

**What they actually mean**

"I'm ready to buy but I want risk reversal. I want a way out if I'm wrong." This is actually a positive signal — they're negotiating, not objecting. Buyers who are checking the exit before they enter are buyers.

---

**The WRONG response**

> "We don't currently offer refunds, but our customer service is great—"

You've closed the door right before they were going to walk through it. The risk question is a buying question.

---

**The Hormozi reframe**

"Good question, and I want to give you a direct answer.

Here's our philosophy on this: the 14-day trial is fully free. No charge. You get complete Pro access — all the federal and SLED matches, AI features, exports, everything. If you run 14 days and don't see a single opportunity worth bidding on for your company, don't upgrade. That's the guarantee.

After you upgrade, if something's wrong — tool isn't working, data is wrong, we screwed something up — we fix it or refund it. We don't want money for a product that isn't delivering value.

What I can't guarantee is that you'll win a contract. No tool can. What I can show you is the pipeline that exists for companies with your NAICS codes and certifications right now — and you can judge whether that pipeline is worth $89/mo to track."

---

**Follow-up question to take control**

"If you run the 14-day trial and you see ten HOT matches for your profile, is $89/mo worth it to keep them coming?"

*(This converts the risk question into a conditional yes.)*

---

**Downsell path**

Not needed here. The trial IS the risk reversal. Lead hard with it.
