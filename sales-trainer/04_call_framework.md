# CapturePilot — Intro/Discovery/Close Call Framework
### Hormozi + Sandler hybrid | 30–35 min | Updated 2026-06-09

---

## How to Use This Document

Run every intro call against this framework. Phases are time-boxed so a 30-minute slot lands a decision — not a vague follow-up. Read the WHY blocks once, internalize them, then work from the question lists and the cheatsheet at the bottom.

**The one rule that overrides everything else:** you are a busy person evaluating whether this contractor is a fit for a limited-seat platform. You are not a vendor begging for a deal. The moment you break that frame — by over-explaining, by defending price before they ask, by filling silences — you hand control to them and the call becomes a free consulting session.

---

## Phase 1 — Opening (0–2 min)

### Goal
Interrupt the script they're running in their head ("sales pitch incoming, smile and deflect"), establish that this is a two-way evaluation, and get permission to ask direct questions.

### Never say
- "How are you today?" / "Hope you're having a great week!"
- "Thanks so much for making time."
- "I'm really excited to show you what we've built."
- "So let me just walk you through the platform real quick."

### Always say — pick one of three openers

**Opener A (pattern interrupt — direct):**
> "Hey [Name] — I've got about 30 minutes blocked. Here's how I usually run these: I'm going to ask you some pointed questions about where you are with federal contracting, you do the same to me, and then we figure out together whether CapturePilot makes sense for you. Sound fair?"

**Opener B (frame-setter — qualification language):**
> "Before I show you anything, I want to make sure this is actually worth your time. A lot of companies we talk to aren't a good fit — wrong stage, wrong certifications, whatever. So I'm going to ask you a few blunt questions up front. If it looks like there's a match, I'll show you exactly what we do. If not, I'll tell you and we can wrap early. Good?"

**Opener C (referral / warm intro variant):**
> "[Name], [referral source] told me a little about your situation so I've got some context. I want to make sure that context is right before we go anywhere. Can I ask you a couple quick questions before I say anything about the platform?"

### One-line agenda (always deliver this)
> "Plan is: I understand your situation, you understand what we do, we decide at the end if next steps make sense. Thirty minutes max."

### Why this works
The traditional opener ("How are you? / Let me tell you about us") tells the prospect they're in a sales call. They go into deflection mode. The pattern interrupt signals that you're different, that you might say no to them, and that there's something worth qualifying for. That creates curiosity and drops their guard faster than any rapport-building small talk. Thirty minutes is also a commitment signal — you're busy enough to have hard stops.

## Phase 2 — Sandler Pain-Funnel Discovery (2–12 min)

### Goal
Surface real pain, quantify it in dollars and time, project it forward so they feel the cost of inaction, and get them to say — in their own words — what solving it would be worth. You are not selling yet. You are diagnosing.

### The funnel logic (run it in order)
1. **Surface** — get them talking about frustration
2. **Cost** — tie it to money or time they can name
3. **Project** — make the future feel worse without a fix
4. **Budget** — let them put a number on the solution before you name a price

### Discovery questions (verbatim — pick 8–10, always run #1 and #7)

**1. The opener (always use this first):**
> "What's the most frustrating thing about chasing federal work right now?"
*(Then shut up. Let them talk for 60+ seconds. Don't interrupt.)*

**2. Current process:**
> "Walk me through exactly how you find new bids today. Like, step by step, what does that actually look like?"

**3. Volume problem:**
> "How many hours a week do you or someone on your team spend just looking for opportunities — not writing, not qualifying, just looking?"

**4. Win rate:**
> "Out of the last ten opportunities you bid on, how many did you win?"

**5. Pipeline visibility:**
> "Right now, if I asked you what's in your federal pipeline for the next 90 days — could you tell me, or would you have to go digging?"

**6. Missed opportunities:**
> "Has there been a contract you found out about after the deadline — where if you'd known a week earlier you probably would have bid?"

**7. Cost-of-pain (always use this):**
> "Let's put a number on it. If you won one more mid-size contract this year — say, $500K — what does that do to your revenue? And what do you think it's costing you right now that you're not winning that?"

**8. Set-aside leverage:**
> "You've got [certification]. Are you using that as a competitive wedge on set-asides, or is most of your pipeline full-and-open competition?"

**9. Competitor awareness:**
> "Do you know who's winning the contracts you're losing? Like, are you tracking incumbents?"

**10. Future pain:**
> "If nothing changes — same process, same results — where are you in 12 months? Still looking manually, same win rate?"

**11. Pain budget:**
> "Hypothetically, if a tool handled all the opportunity research, ranked everything for you, and even drafted the proposals — what would you be willing to pay per month for that? Don't worry about our price, I'm just curious what you'd pay."

**12. Status quo cost:**
> "What are you spending right now on capture — tools, time, consultants, anything?"

### Words to never use during discovery
- "Great question."
- "Absolutely."
- "That's really interesting — so what we do is..."
*(Do not pivot to your solution mid-discovery. You don't have enough information yet. And prospects can smell a pivot.)*

### Note-taking signal
Take notes visibly. Say: "Mind if I write some of this down? I want to make sure I map the right things to what I show you." This signals you're listening, not waiting to talk.

### Why this works
Sandler's pain funnel works because people don't buy solutions — they buy relief from pain they've personally named. The cost-of-pain question (#7) is critical because it anchors value in their language before your price lands. The future-pain question (#10) triggers loss aversion — the most powerful motivator in B2B sales. The pain-budget question (#11) gives you a ceiling to work within and often reveals their real budget better than "what's your budget?" ever will.

## Phase 3 — Qualification (12–17 min)

### Goal
Confirm they are a real prospect before you invest another 15 minutes. A bad prospect who stays on the line costs you two future good-prospect slots. Getting a graceful no here is better than getting a ghost after a demo.

### Qualifying questions (in order)

**Q1 — SAM registration:**
> "Are you currently registered in SAM.gov with an active UEI?"
*(If no: "Okay — we should probably get you registered first. That's actually a free process and takes about a week. Once you're in, then we can talk about CapturePilot. Want me to send you the registration checklist?")*

**Q2 — Certification status:**
> "What SBA certifications are you holding right now? SDVOSB, 8(a), HUBZone, WOSB — anything active?"
*(No certification isn't a dealbreaker, but note it — shifts the value prop to full-and-open competition. If they have 8(a) or HUBZone, you have a stronger set-aside angle.)*

**Q3 — Prior federal revenue:**
> "Have you won federal work before, or are you trying to break in?"
*(Total greenfields are a harder sell — realistic 90-day win probability is low; they need pipeline-building framing, not quick-win framing.)*

**Q4 — NAICS clarity:**
> "Do you know your primary NAICS code off the top of your head?"
*(If they don't know: it's not disqualifying, but they haven't set up SAM.gov correctly — note as onboarding friction.)*

**Q5 — Decision authority:**
> "Is it just you making this call, or do you need to loop someone else in before you could move forward?"
*(If there's a partner/spouse/CFO: "Totally fine — would it make sense to have them on a follow-up call? I'd rather give the full picture once than repeat it twice." Do NOT proceed to close without the real decision-maker on the line.)*

**Q6 — Investment readiness (Hormozi-style — NEVER ask "what's your budget?"):**
> "We've got plans from $39/month up to done-for-you retainers. I'm not going to push you toward either until I know what you actually need. But I want to ask it straight: are you ready to make an investment right now to fix this, or are you still in the kicking-the-tires phase?"
*(If "kicking the tires": "That's fine — what would you need to see to feel confident enough to move forward?" This surfaces the real objection early.)*

**Q7 — Timeline:**
> "Is there a contract deadline coming up that's creating urgency, or is this more of a 'build for the next 6 months' situation?"
*(Urgency = prioritize speed-to-value in demo. Long-horizon = lean into pipeline-building ROI narrative.)*

### Disqualification threshold
If they are: (a) not SAM-registered, (b) no certifications + no prior federal wins + no real budget signal, (c) not the decision-maker and the DM won't join a call — gracefully exit.

> "Honestly, I don't want to waste your time. Based on what you've told me, [specific gap] means we wouldn't be able to get you real results in 90 days. Let me send you [registration checklist / SAM.gov setup guide / free Field Manual], and reach back out when you're at that point."

Ending a call 20 minutes early with a clean no is better than a 3-week ghost.

### Why this works
Qualification before demo is the Sandler rule that most salespeople break because it feels rude. It isn't. A prospect who gets through a 20-minute demo that was never for them feels their time was wasted — and they blame you. Qualifying hard up front signals confidence and respects their time. The investment-readiness question (Q6) is lifted from Hormozi's "$100M Offers" framing — it forces self-selection between buyers and browsers before you reveal price.

## Phase 4 — Value Demonstration (17–25 min)

### Goal
Show them — specifically — how CapturePilot solves the three pains they named in Phase 2. This is NOT a feature tour. It is a pain-to-solution mapping exercise. If they didn't mention competitor tracking as a pain, don't spend three minutes on the competitor tab.

### The setup line (always use this to open the demo)
> "Okay. Based on what you told me — [playback their top 2-3 pains verbatim, e.g. 'you're spending about 8 hours a week just looking, you missed a bid last fall that you would have won, and you have no visibility into who's beating you'] — let me show you the three things that directly address that. I'm going to skip a lot of the platform because it won't matter until you've seen these."

### Pain-to-feature mapping (match to what they said)

| They said... | Show them... | The line to use |
|---|---|---|
| "I spend hours on SAM.gov every week" | Match feed + HOT/WARM/COLD ranking | "This runs every day at 2am against your profile. You open this in the morning, you see your ranked list. That's it." |
| "I miss deadlines / find out too late" | Deadline alert + email digest | "You'll get an email the morning a high-scoring opportunity hits. Not a blast — just the ones that score over your threshold." |
| "I don't know who I'm competing against" | Competitor profiles tab | "Type in any company you know is bidding against you. You'll see their NAICS overlap, what agencies they're winning, their SAM certifications." |
| "Proposals take forever" | AI Proposal Writer | "You pick the opportunity, it reads the solicitation, and it drafts the full proposal structure. You edit — you don't write from scratch." |
| "I'm not sure what certifications give me an edge" | Set-aside filter + HOT match logic | "Your [certification] is already in your profile. Every set-aside opportunity that matches it scores higher automatically." |
| "I don't know if state/local is worth chasing" | SLED tab (Pro only) | "We pull bids from 48 states — county, city, state agency. Same scoring engine, but for everything below the federal line." |
| "I've got 3 people in BD but we're disorganized" | Pipeline Kanban + team seats | "Your team all logs into the same workspace. You can see who's working what opportunity, at what stage, without a spreadsheet." |

### Demo guardrails
- Maximum 3 features per call. Pick the ones mapped to their pains.
- Narrate from outcomes, not features: "What this means for you is..." not "And here you can see the..."
- Do not click into every sub-panel. Show the surface, describe the depth, move on.
- If they ask "can it do X?": answer truthfully. If no: "Not yet — but here's how people handle that today."

### The 30-day picture (always close the demo with this)
> "Here's what the first 30 days actually looks like. Week 1: we set up your profile with your NAICS codes, certifications, and target states — takes about 20 minutes. Week 2: your first batch of HOT matches comes through and you see whether the scoring feels right. Week 3: if you're Pro or Agencies, you're running your first AI proposal draft. By day 30, you have a working pipeline with real bids in it. That's the goal."

### Why this works
The average software demo kills deals because it shows everything the tool can do and leaves the prospect deciding what's relevant. Feature soup creates confusion and delays decisions. Mapping features to the exact pains they named creates an "of course" moment — they feel heard, and the product becomes an obvious answer to a problem they've already articulated. The 30-day picture replaces abstract promises with a concrete sequence, which makes the decision feel low-risk.

## Phase 5 — Offer + Close (25–30 min)

### Goal
Present the right plan anchored to what they told you, get a yes or a hard no, and never leave the call with a "let me think about it."

### The anchor setup (say this before the price)
> "Before I tell you the number, I want to anchor it to what you said earlier — [their cost-of-pain number]. If one extra mid-size contract is worth [their number] to you, the math on the tool has to make sense against that. Keep that in mind."

### The plan presentation

Present TWO options. Never one (feels like a take-it-or-leave-it). Never three (creates analysis paralysis). Label them based on what they need — not by tier name.

**For a solo operator (Pro is almost always the right fit):**
> "Based on what you've told me, there are really two ways to go. Option one is the self-service Pro plan — $89 a month, or $71 if you pay annually. That gets you unlimited federal matches, SLED, the AI Proposal Writer, competitor tracking, exports, everything. You run it yourself, we're here for support. Option two is our done-for-you consulting tier — starts at $2,500 a month and our team does the capture work for you: CO emails, proposals, strategy calls, all of it. Given that you said [specific thing they said about their bandwidth], which one actually fits where you are right now?"

**For an agency/consultancy:**
> "You've got two real paths. One is the Pro plan — $89 a month per workspace. Works if you're managing a handful of clients and want the tool for yourself. The other is our Agencies plan — custom pricing, but starts around $399 a month — multi-client workspace, white-label, GPT-4o on every feature, dedicated support. Given that you're running [X clients], which one fits?"

### The assumptive close (say this — word for word)
> "Which one makes more sense given what you told me?"

Then stop talking. Completely. Wait. Do not fill the silence. The first person to speak after the price loses leverage. If the silence hits 30 seconds, let it run. They are thinking. That is exactly what you want.

### If they say yes
> "Perfect. Here's what happens next: I'll send you a Stripe link in the next 5 minutes. Once you're in, you'll get the onboarding email with a direct Calendly link to schedule your profile setup call. We'll get your NAICS codes and certifications locked in on that call and your first HOT matches start hitting by morning. Any questions before I send the link?"

### If they say "let me think about it" — do not accept this
"Let me think about it" is almost always: (a) a price objection they won't say, (b) a decision-authority issue they didn't disclose, or (c) they didn't see the value in the demo. Diagnose it.

> "I appreciate that. Before you go — can I ask what specifically you need to think through? I'd rather answer it now than have you sit with a half-answered question for a week."

If they name it: handle it directly (see Phase 5 — Objection Handling below).
If they won't name it: get a specific next step.

> "Okay. Can we do this — I'll send you the deck, and let's put 20 minutes on the calendar for [specific day] to answer whatever came up. Does Thursday at 2pm work?"

If they won't schedule: they are a no. Don't chase.

### Objection handling — the most common five

**"It's too expensive."**
> "Compared to what? You told me you're spending [X hours] a week on SAM.gov. If your time is worth [their hourly rate] — which is conservative for a business owner — you're already spending $[rough math] a month on the problem we're solving. This is cheaper than your current process."

**"We're not ready yet."**
> "What would need to be true for you to be ready? I'm not asking to push you — I genuinely want to know, because that tells me whether this is a timing issue or a fit issue."

**"I need to show my partner/wife/team."**
> "Makes total sense. Two options: I can put together a one-pager for them, or we schedule a 20-minute call with all of you on it so I can answer questions directly. Which is easier?"

**"We just lost a contract, tight on cash."**
> "I hear you. That's exactly the kind of moment where the pipeline question matters most — you need new opportunities in faster. The annual plan locks in at $71/month, which is less than most people spend on proposal software that doesn't even find the bids. Is the annual price more workable, or do you need to wait a specific number of weeks?"

**"I tried something like this before and it didn't work."**
> "Tell me what didn't work. Because that either means the other tool had a gap, or there was a setup issue, or the timing was wrong. I want to know which it was — if it's a fundamental gap I can't fill, I'd rather tell you now."

### Why the silence rule matters
Hormozi's data, backed by decades of Sandler training: salespeople who talk after the price get lower close rates because they start pre-emptively defending the number before the buyer has even objected. Silence lets the prospect process. Processing leads to decisions. Talking leads to more questions.

## Phase 6 — Close-Out (30–35 min)

### Goal
Lock in a clear next step for every call. There are three acceptable outcomes: yes, scheduled follow-up with agenda, or clean no. There is no fourth outcome.

### If YES — immediate next steps
1. Send Stripe link in the chat or to their email during the call. Do it while they're still on.
2. Confirm email address for the Stripe receipt + welcome sequence.
3. Schedule the onboarding/profile setup call before hanging up.
4. Say: "You'll get a welcome email in about 5 minutes. The most important thing to do today is complete the profile setup in the onboarding flow — takes 15 minutes. Everything else flows from that."

### If MAYBE — scheduled follow-up (only if they named a specific reason)
Requirements for a "real maybe":
- They have a named decision-maker who wasn't on the call (and will be on the follow-up)
- They named a specific question or objection that has a real answer
- They agreed to a specific date and time (not "end of the week")

If all three are true:
> "I'll hold that slot. I'll send a calendar invite and a short agenda so [partner's name / your team] can review the key points before we meet. Sound good?"

Then send the invite from your calendar within 5 minutes. If they don't confirm the invite in 24 hours, they're a no.

### If NO — learn and close cleanly
Do not end on defeat. Ask one more question:
> "I appreciate your honesty — most people won't say no directly. Can I ask: was it a fit issue, a timing issue, or a price issue? Even a sentence helps me."

If price: "Do you want me to send the Field Manual? It's a free guide on finding federal contracts manually — no strings attached."
If timing: "Mind if I check back in 90 days? I'll put it in the calendar — no pitch, just a check-in."
If fit: "Fair. Any referrals you can think of who'd be a better fit? We pay a finder's fee."

### The banned move
> "I'll send you the deck and circle back in a few weeks."

This is how leads die. If they're not ready to schedule a follow-up now, the deck won't change that. Sending the deck is a security blanket for salespeople who don't want to accept a no. Kill this habit.

### Why clean no's matter
A clean no frees 45 minutes per week you'd otherwise spend chasing ghosts. It also preserves your credibility with that prospect — if their situation changes in 90 days, they'll remember you respected their answer. Salespeople who push through nos train their prospects to ghost them.

## Objection Bank (Reference — not part of call flow)

Use these when objections arise in Phase 5 or Phase 6.

| Objection | Root cause | Response |
|---|---|---|
| "I'm already using GovWin / BGov / Sam.gov direct" | Tool switching friction | "Those tools show you everything. We show you your stuff — ranked for you specifically. What's your hit rate on what GovWin surfaces?" |
| "We're a subcontractor, not a prime" | Wrong stage | "We have prime contractors who are actively looking to team. Your NAICS and certifications are searchable by them in our partner directory. This might actually be faster than going direct." |
| "We're international / not a US company" | Disqualify | "We're only licensed for US-registered entities on SAM.gov. That's a hard constraint on the data we use. I can't make that work for you." |
| "We don't have NAICS codes" | Setup gap | "That's the first thing we fix in onboarding. It takes about 10 minutes to identify your primary and secondary NAICS codes. That's not a blocker." |
| "Can I try it for free?" | Risk aversion | "Yes — we have a 14-day full Pro trial built into signup. No call required for that. But it's worth 5 more minutes here so I can make sure your profile is set up to actually match well from day one." |
| "This feels like a lot to learn" | Adoption fear | "The onboarding call is 30 minutes. Most users have their first HOT matches by the next morning. It's not a complex tool — it's a daily digest with a ranked list." |
