# 02 — Ideal Customer Profile & Core Pains

> **Audience for this doc**: Sales partners, SDRs, onboarding specialists, and AI sales agents running CapturePilot outreach. Use this document to qualify leads fast, skip the wrong conversations, and zero in on the 7 pains that convert.

---

## 1. Dead-Center ICP

### One-Paragraph Portrait

The perfect CapturePilot customer is a U.S. small business owner — typically the founder, President, or a part-time BD director they've hired — running a 5-to-50-person services firm with between $500K and $15M in annual revenue. They are already SAM.gov-registered with a valid 12-character UEI and hold at least one SBA set-aside certification (SDVOSB/VOSB, 8(a), HUBZone, WOSB/EDWOSB, or Tribal 8(a)) or are actively pursuing one. Their core business falls inside federal services categories: janitorial and facilities management (NAICS 561720, 561210), IT services and cybersecurity (541511, 541512, 541519), construction and specialty trades (236220, 238xx), engineering (541330), or management and admin consulting (541611, 541618). They have won 1–5 prime contracts already — or they are currently sub-contracting through a prime and want to go direct — and their ceiling isn't skills, it's pipeline visibility. Before CapturePilot they were spending 5–10 hours every week manually combing SAM.gov, setting up Google alerts on agency names, and forwarding opportunities to themselves in email chains. They have a real company website (not a Wix placeholder) and a business email on a custom domain. They were founded 3 or more years ago, so the frustration is genuine — they've bid, they've lost, and they want to know why and what to bid next.

## 2. The 7 Pains We Resolve

---

### Pain 1 — The SAM.gov Firehose Problem

**Description.** SAM.gov publishes 400–600 new opportunities per day. Without profile-based filtering, a small contractor drowns in irrelevant awards — DoD R&D solicitations, large-business-only IDIQs, agencies the contractor has never worked with. Manually screening for relevance takes hours and still misses things.

**Prospect quote:** "I log into SAM.gov every morning and run my keyword searches, but half of what comes back is garbage — wrong NAICS, wrong set-aside, dollar amounts we can never compete on. By the time I've filtered through it, the window for a sources-sought response is already two days ago."

**What CapturePilot does.** Ingests and scores 40,000+ federal opportunities daily against the contractor's exact NAICS codes, SBA certifications, target states, and deal-size range. Every opportunity arrives pre-ranked HOT/WARM/COLD with an explanation — the user knows in 90 seconds whether something is worth reading. No manual filter setup, no missed windows.

**Anti-pain check.** If the prospect says: "We already have a dedicated capture analyst who handles SAM.gov full-time" — they are either above the ICP ceiling (too big) or a candidate for the Agency tier, not Pro.

---

### Pain 2 — Invisible State and Local Opportunities

**Description.** A significant portion of government spend for services firms (facilities, janitorial, construction, IT services) flows through state agencies, counties, and cities — not the federal SAM.gov feed. Most contractors have no systematic way to monitor these, even though SLED contracts often have fewer competitors and less red tape.

**Prospect quote:** "I heard the county was putting out a five-year facilities management contract — I only found out because a friend texted me. By the time I downloaded the RFP from the state portal I'd missed the pre-bid conference."

**What CapturePilot does.** Pulls SLED (state, local, education) bids from 48 states via HigherGov integration, Bonfire portal scrapers, and OpenGov connectors — all surfaced in the same match feed as federal opportunities, filtered by the contractor's states and NAICS. No separate portal logins, no manual monitoring.

**Anti-pain check.** If the prospect says: "We only do federal work — we have no interest in state or local" — this is a Light-tier candidate, not Pro. Acknowledge it and sell down. Do not try to force the SLED value prop.

---

### Pain 3 — Proposal Writing Bottleneck

**Description.** Federal proposals follow strict formatting conventions (FAR clauses, PWS response structure, evaluation factor matrix, past performance narratives). Writing a competitive response from scratch takes 20–40 hours. Small firms either skip opportunities because of writing time or submit thin responses that lose on technical evaluation score.

**Prospect quote:** "We found a perfect HUBZone small business set-aside for our NAICS. We have the past performance, the certifications, everything. But I spent three weekends on the proposal and it still came back at 68 out of 100 on technical. We don't have time to write well on every bid."

**What CapturePilot does.** AI Proposal Writer (100 proposals/mo on Pro) generates full draft responses — executive summary, technical approach, past performance narratives, capability alignment — trained on the contractor's own capability statement and the opportunity's PWS/SOW. The result is a structured first draft in under 10 minutes, not 40 hours. The contractor reviews and signs, not writes from scratch.

**Anti-pain check.** If the prospect says: "We hire a proposal writer on retainer — she handles all of this for us" — the proposal writer may actually be a champion user for CapturePilot (saves her time), not a disqualifier. Reframe and ask to loop her in.

---

### Pain 4 — No Competitive Intelligence on Incumbents

**Description.** The hardest federal bids to win are recompetes where a large incumbent has 3–5 years of relationship equity with the CO. Small contractors bid against incumbents they've never researched — they don't know the incumbent's pricing history, past performance ratings, team size, or certifications. They price too high or too low and lose blind.

**Prospect quote:** "We keep losing to the same two companies on DoD facility contracts. I don't even know who they are — I can't find their pricing, I don't know how they're positioned, I'm just guessing."

**What CapturePilot does.** Competitor profiles built from 80K+ SAM.gov-registered contractors — services, leadership, past clients, NAICS coverage, certifications, federal award history. The Recompete Radar flags opportunities where the current incumbent's contract is expiring within 90–180 days, so the contractor can begin capture positioning before RFP drop instead of reacting after.

**Anti-pain check.** If the prospect says: "We mostly pursue sole-source or 8(a) direct awards — we don't compete on open bids that much" — competitive intelligence is lower priority for them. Lean into the opportunity matching and proposal writer instead.

---

### Pain 5 — Capability Statement Is Out of Date or Nonexistent

**Description.** A capability statement is required for sources-sought responses, industry days, and teaming conversations. Most small contractors have either a stale Word document that hasn't been updated since their first contract, or nothing at all. Without a strong, current cap statement, they can't respond to RFIs or introduce themselves to primes, which cuts them out of the earliest and most valuable part of the procurement cycle.

**Prospect quote:** "My capability statement is a three-page Word doc I wrote in 2021. It doesn't have our new NAICS codes, it doesn't have half our past performance, and it definitely doesn't reflect the AI language that agencies are asking for now. Every time I try to update it I get stuck and never finish."

**What CapturePilot does.** Voice-powered Capability Statement Builder — speak or paste a description of your company, past performance, and differentiators, and the AI produces a properly structured, FAR-aligned capability statement. TipTap rich-text editor for manual refinement, PDF export with branded header, Google Drive save, and file upload if they already have a version they want to improve.

**Anti-pain check.** If the prospect says: "We have a professional BD firm that keeps our cap statement current" — they are likely in the upper ICP or Agency tier. Confirm who manages that relationship and whether CapturePilot could replace or augment it.

---

### Pain 6 — Finding Teaming Partners Is a Networking Lottery

**Description.** Large-agency task orders and BIC MACs require team composition — a prime with a few specialty subs, or a small prime who needs a large-business partner for bonding or past performance. Right now, teaming is 90% conference networking and LinkedIn cold messages. Contractors end up with the same 2–3 partner relationships they've had for years and miss teaming windows on new agencies.

**Prospect quote:** "I know I need a WOSB partner for this HHS set-aside but I don't know who to call. I've reached out to three companies on LinkedIn and none of them responded. I'm going to miss the bid because I can't field the team in time."

**What CapturePilot does.** Teaming Partner Directory searches 80K+ SAM.gov-registered contractors by NAICS overlap, certifications (8(a), HUBZone, WOSB, SDVOSB), state, and size — filtered to companies that have past performance in the same categories. The Certified Teaming tab surfaces SBA-certified 8(a)/HUBZone/Tribal firms with SBLO contact info at top-tier primes (Lockheed, Leidos, BAE, GDIT, Booz Allen) for sub opportunities.

**Anti-pain check.** If the prospect says: "We already have established teaming relationships and don't need to find new partners" — skip the teaming value prop. This contractor is likely in the pipeline and proposal writer segment instead.

---

### Pain 7 — They Don't Know What They Don't Know (Market Blindness)

**Description.** Small contractors often pursue the same agency year after year without knowing that a different agency — one that fits their NAICS perfectly — is obligating 3x the spend and has an incumbent contract expiring in 6 months. They have no market intelligence layer, so their "strategy" is reactive: bid what shows up, not what's strategically winnable.

**Prospect quote:** "We've been going after DHS janitor contracts for four years. Our win rate is terrible. I had no idea the VA was spending twice as much on facilities management in our states until someone told me at a conference. We've been hunting in the wrong pond."

**What CapturePilot does.** Dashboard market intelligence — per-NAICS spend rollups by agency, Year-End Spend Radar (unobligated balance by agency flagged by NAICS overlap), Recompete Radar for expiring incumbencies, and FPDS past award data to identify where the real dollars flow. Contractors can see in one screen which agencies are spending the most in their categories and shift hunting accordingly.

**Anti-pain check.** If the prospect says: "We have a GovTribe or Bloomberg Government account and use that for market intel" — they are a sophisticated buyer. Acknowledge those tools, then position CapturePilot as the action layer on top (matching + proposals + SLED), not a replacement for their research stack.

## 3. The 5 Anti-Personas (Who to Eject)

These prospect types look like buyers but are not. Spending time on them blocks real pipeline.

---

### Anti-Persona 1 — "I Just Want Grants"

**Pattern:** "We're a non-profit / social enterprise / community organization. We need help finding federal grants for our programs."

**Why they don't fit.** CapturePilot is a federal contracting and procurement platform, not a grants database. Grants.gov is a separate universe with different solicitation types, compliance requirements, and institutional eligibility rules. CapturePilot ingests SAM.gov procurement notices, not cooperative agreements or assistance listings in the grants domain.

**Ejection script.** "CapturePilot is built for companies that sell goods or services to government agencies under contract — not for grant recipients. For grants you'd want Grants.gov directly or a grants management platform like eCivis or Submittable. If you ever do move into contracting — selling services under a government contract — we'd be a great fit. Happy to reconnect then."

---

### Anti-Persona 2 — "Write My Proposal For Me"

**Pattern:** "I don't want software. I want someone to write the proposal — I don't have time to be in a tool."

**Why they don't fit (for self-serve).** They are describing the Consulting / Done-For-You tier, not Pro. Selling them Pro will end in churn on day 30 when they realize they still have to use the tool.

**Routing protocol.** This is NOT an ejection — it's a routing. Pass to the Consulting/DFY path via Americurial. The signal is: "sounds like what you're describing is our done-for-you service — our team handles all of this for you directly. Let me connect you with that team." Do NOT try to convince them to use software if they've explicitly said they won't.

**True ejection criteria.** If they say DFY and then push back on a $2,500+/mo retainer — eject gracefully. "Our managed service starts at $2,500/month on a 12-month engagement. If that's outside your budget right now, the Pro plan at $89/mo with our AI proposal writer gets you 80% of the way there on your own schedule."

---

### Anti-Persona 3 — "I Want to Learn GovCon First"

**Pattern:** "I'm just starting out, I haven't registered on SAM.gov yet, I'm not sure which NAICS codes to pick. Can CapturePilot teach me how to do government contracting?"

**Why they don't fit (yet).** CapturePilot is an operational tool, not a GovCon curriculum. Signal 1 and Signal 4 from the ICP checklist are absent — no UEI, no past performance. The Quick Company Check and Free tier can give them a taste, but selling them Pro before they have the fundamentals will cause churn and a bad experience.

**Routing protocol.** Free tier or the CapturePilot blog. "Before you get full value out of the platform, you'll want to get your SAM.gov registration active and pick your NAICS codes — that usually takes 2–4 weeks for SAM.gov to process. In the meantime, run your company through our free Quick Company Check at capturepilot.com/check — it'll show you what certifications you're eligible for and which opportunities already match your profile. Once your SAM registration clears, come back and we'll set you up on a trial."

---

### Anti-Persona 4 — "We Only Do SBIR / Research Grants"

**Pattern:** "We're a deep-tech company. We do Phase I and Phase II SBIR awards. We don't do services contracts."

**Why they don't fit.** SBIR program solicitations are a specialized procurement track managed by agency-specific R&D offices (DoD SBIR/STTR, NIH SBIR, etc.). CapturePilot does surface SBIR solicitations via the SBIR.gov integration, but the full platform (SLED, proposal writer calibrated for commercial/services, teaming partner directory, capability statement builder) is built around services contracting, not R&D solicitations. The value prop is mostly mismapped.

**Ejection script.** "The platform does pull SBIR solicitations from SBIR.gov and you can monitor those, but about 80% of what CapturePilot does is built for services and construction contractors, not R&D firms. You'd likely use maybe 20% of the platform. If SBIR pipeline management is your primary need, you'd get more value from a tool like SBIR.gov's native tracker or a program-specific consultant."

---

### Anti-Persona 5 — "I Already Have a Capture Team and a BD Platform"

**Pattern:** "We have a dedicated capture manager, a proposal manager, a BD database on Salesforce GovCon, and we already use GovTribe or Bloomberg Gov. I'm looking to see what else is out there."

**Why they don't fit (for Pro/self-serve).** They are at or above the ICP ceiling. This is not a bad prospect — it's a misrouted one. The right conversation is Agencies & Consultants tier or a strategic enterprise discussion, not Pro at $89/mo.

**Routing protocol.** "It sounds like you're running a mature capture operation — at that scale you'd be looking at our Agencies & Consultants tier, which adds multi-client workspace, white-label, GPT-4o on all AI features, bulk proposal generation, and a dedicated CSM. That conversation involves a custom quote — would it make sense to schedule a 30-minute intro call to map out whether there's a fit?"

## 4. The 3 Tiers of ICP Fit

Use these tiers to determine which CapturePilot offer to close on — not whether to sell at all.

---

### GREEN — Close on Pilot (High Readiness)

**Who they are.** Active SAM.gov registrant, 1–3 SBA certs, 5–50 employees, 2+ prime contracts in past performance, currently spending 5+ hours/week on manual opportunity research, owner or VP-of-BD on the call, can make a $89–$399/mo decision in the meeting.

**Behavioral signals.**
- References a specific opportunity they recently lost or missed
- Has an existing (if outdated) capability statement
- Knows their NAICS codes off the top of their head
- Company website shows real service lines and past clients
- Business email on custom domain

**What to close.** **Pro at $89/mo** — start the 14-day trial in the call, walk through NAICS setup together. If they have 3+ employees doing capture work and ask about seat sharing, introduce the Agencies & Consultants tier discussion. Target time-to-close: same call or follow-up within 48 hours.

**Urgency levers.** "The recompete radar already shows three VA facility contracts in [their state] expiring in the next 90 days — you'd see them the moment your trial activates." OR: "There's a sources-sought for [their NAICS] at [relevant agency] that closes in 11 days — let's get you in before that closes."

---

### YELLOW — Close on Co-Pilot (Middle Readiness)

**Who they are.** SAM.gov-registered, certifications in progress or recently obtained, 1–2 years in federal contracting, limited past performance (1 contract or sub-only history), willing to invest but asking "is this worth it for us right now?" Likely 3–15 employees.

**Behavioral signals.**
- Says "we're just getting started in federal" but has clearly done the SAM registration and UEI work
- Has bid once or twice but didn't win
- Needs help knowing which opportunities to chase, not just finding them
- On the call but slightly skeptical — needs to see a win scenario
- May ask "can I see this in action before committing?"

**What to close.** Start with the **14-day Pro trial** (card required) and personally walk them through the onboarding — plug in NAICS, certifications, states, and show them their first HOT matches live. If they hesitate on the card, offer the **Light tier at $39/mo** as a no-commitment entry point with an explicit upgrade path once they see a bid worth writing. Target time-to-close: trial activation in the call; card or upgrade within 7 days.

**Coaching note.** Yellow prospects churn if they don't get a quick win. Assign them the Quick Win Action: "Within 72 hours, respond to one sources-sought notice — even a 2-paragraph email response. That's your first step toward a recompete advantage on that contract." Give them the path, not just the tool.

---

### RED — Downsell to Self-Service or the Field Manual (Low Readiness, Real Intent)

**Who they are.** Real intent to pursue government contracts but not operationally ready: SAM.gov registration in progress, no certs yet, no past performance, under 5 employees, or budget constraint is genuine (not negotiating). They are not wasting your time — they are 6–12 months away from being a GREEN prospect.

**Behavioral signals.**
- SAM.gov registration is pending or they're "working on it"
- Revenue is sub-$500K or pre-revenue in GovCon
- No capability statement, no past performance
- Single founder with no BD capacity
- Monthly budget ceiling under $89 (they name it explicitly)

**What to do.**
1. Register them on the **Free tier** — Quick Company Check, NAICS identification, basic eligibility report. No card.
2. Send them the CapturePilot Field Manual (blog / resource center) — specifically the SAM.gov registration guide and NAICS selection walkthrough.
3. Set a 90-day follow-up. "When your SAM registration clears and you've completed your first sources-sought response, come back — your first month on Pro is on us."
4. Do NOT try to close Pro today. RED prospects who buy Pro under pressure cancel within 30 days, generate chargebacks, and leave bad reviews.

**Exception.** If a RED prospect has real revenue ($1M+) but is new to federal and just operationally unready — consider routing them to the **Consulting/DFY** path. They have the budget; they just need a team to do it for them while they get up the learning curve.
