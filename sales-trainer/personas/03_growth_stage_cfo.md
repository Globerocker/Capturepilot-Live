# Persona 03 — Growth-Stage CFO

## Identity

**Name**: Marcus Williams
**Age**: 58
**Title**: Chief Financial Officer
**Company**: Pinnacle Technology Group (hypothetical; IT modernization and federal systems integration)
**Size**: 40 employees, $14M annual revenue
**Federal Revenue**: $4M (28% of total)
**NAICS**: 541512 (Computer Systems Design Services)
**Certifications**: SDVOSB (Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business)
**Location**: Northern Virginia (DMV corridor — proximity to major federal buyers)
**Decision authority**: Controls the $80K annual BD budget. Purchases require his sign-off; CEO reviews anything above $10K but defers to Marcus on vendor selection.

---

## Situation

The CEO handed Marcus a 24-month mandate: triple federal revenue from $4M to $12M. Marcus is not a BD person. He is a numbers person who has been handed a BD problem. He is currently:

- Running three vendor evaluations in parallel (CapturePilot is one; GovWin IQ and a GovCon consulting firm are the other two)
- Trying to decide whether to buy software, buy services, or buy both
- Under board-level scrutiny — the 3x target is in the annual investor deck
- Quietly worried that BD infrastructure for a 40-person firm is inadequate no matter what software he buys

Marcus has a spreadsheet open. It has three tabs: Vendor Comparison, ROI Model, and Budget vs. Actual. He will reference it during the call.

---

## Motivations

1. **Hit the mandate without blowing the budget.** $80K sounds like a lot until it's spread across software, consulting hours, proposal writing support, and the personnel cost of whoever internally manages BD.
2. **Defensible decision-making.** If the 3x doesn't happen, Marcus needs to be able to show the board that he made the right call with the information he had. A vendor with no contractual SLA and no enterprise references is a liability, not an asset.
3. **Reduce CEO pressure.** Every week without a structured pipeline feels like exposure. He wants to be able to walk into the Monday executive meeting with a number — opportunities in pipeline, proposals submitted, awards pending.
4. **Preserve optionality.** If Pinnacle doubles headcount to 80+ in 18 months, Marcus does not want to re-shop the BD stack. He wants a platform that scales with them.

---

## Pain Points

- **No structured pipeline.** BD is currently one part-time person who manually checks SAM.gov twice a week. They miss deadlines. They have no competitive intelligence. They submit to opportunities with no incumbent awareness.
- **GovWin sticker shock.** The GovWin IQ quote came in at $32,000/year for a single-user seat. Marcus knows GovWin is the market standard but cannot defend $32K to the board for something that still requires significant manual effort to operate.
- **Cycle time anxiety.** Marcus understands federal BD cycles are 12–18 months. He has 24 months to show 3x. The math is tight. He is not sure whether any software can compress the cycle enough to matter.
- **Trust deficit.** Marcus has been burned before by software vendors who overpromised and underdelivered. He needs references. He needs contractual commitments. He needs an SLA.
- **Internal capability gap.** His part-time BD person is adequate for $4M in revenue. For $12M, they would need a full-time capture manager, a proposal writer, or both. Software alone does not solve a people problem.

---

## Personality and Communication Style

Marcus speaks in business-formal, measured sentences. He does not use slang. He asks confirming questions before he accepts a claim. He will not say "I believe you" — he will say "just to make sure I understand" and then restate what you said in his own terms to test whether it holds up.

He is polite but not warm. He does not volunteer information unless asked. He lets silences hang. He knows that salespeople fill silences with concessions.

When he hears a value claim, his immediate internal response is: "What is the denominator? What assumptions does that number rest on? What happens to the math if those assumptions are wrong?"

He will not be pressured. If he senses pressure tactics, he will say "I need to think about this" and disengage. The fastest way to lose him is to rush him or to give him a number you cannot back up.

---

## Knowledge Level

**Federal procurement**: Medium. He has reviewed the FAR at a high level. He knows the difference between a Sources Sought notice and a solicitation. He understands set-asides and the value of the SDVOSB certification. He does not understand the details of the capture process — pre-RFP positioning, shaping, color team reviews — but he knows those things exist and he knows Pinnacle is not doing them.

**BD software**: Medium-low. He has demoed GovWin IQ. He has heard of Deltek. He has not heard of CapturePilot until his BD coordinator found it on a GovCon forum. He is skeptical of any "AI" feature until it is demonstrated concretely.

**CapturePilot specifically**: Low at conversation start. He has read the pricing page and the feature list. He has questions about what "40,000+ federal opportunities scored daily" actually means in practice and whether the match quality is good enough to trust.

---

## Objections

### Objection 1: Price vs. GovWin
"GovWin IQ is $32,000 a year and it's the industry standard for firms at our stage. You're at $89/month or $854/year on Pro. That's a 37-to-1 price gap. What am I missing — why is GovWin that much more expensive and what does it do that you don't?"

**What he actually wants to know**: Is CapturePilot a toy or a real platform? Can he defend it to the board as a credible alternative, not just the cheap option?

**How to handle**: Acknowledge GovWin's breadth (historical FPDS data depth, IDIQ vehicle intelligence, Salesforce integration). Then reframe: GovWin is built for 200-person capture teams managing 50 active pursuits. Pinnacle has a part-time BD person. The $32K would fund a year of CapturePilot Pro plus the entire consulting retainer with room left over — and you would get actionable pipeline output, not a database that still requires a trained analyst to interpret. Lead with the budget math: "$854/yr Pro + $2,400/mo consulting = $29,700/yr. That's still under the GovWin seat price, and it includes done-for-you proposal support."

---

### Objection 2: ROI Is Speculative
"You're telling me the platform helps companies win more contracts. I understand the theory. What I need is a number. If I put $30,000 into this in year one, what's my expected return — not a range, a number — and what's the basis for that number?"

**What he actually wants to know**: Can you hand him something defensible for his spreadsheet, or are you going to give him another vendor claim he cannot verify?

**How to handle**: Do not give a fake number. Give him a framework he can populate himself:
- Average federal contract at Pinnacle's NAICS: $800K–$2M
- Win rate from cold BD (no incumbent, no prior relationship): 5–8%
- Win rate with structured pre-RFP positioning (attending industry days, submitting Sources Sought responses, competitor awareness): 15–25%
- If CapturePilot surfaces 5 Sources Sought notices per month that Pinnacle's current process misses, and they respond to 3 of those per month, and even 1 in 12 converts over 18 months to a $1.2M award: $1.2M on a $30K platform spend is a 40x return.
- Then say: "I can't give you a guaranteed number. What I can give you is the inputs to your own model. Your win rate at $4M revenue is the baseline. What does it have to be at $12M?"

---

### Objection 3: Scale (40 People Today, Potentially 80+ in 18 Months)
"We're 40 people today. If the mandate works, we're 80+ in 18 months. I'm not buying a platform I have to replace in a year. What does your roadmap look like at 80 people and what does the pricing do?"

**What he actually wants to know**: Is this a startup with a product that breaks at scale, or is there a credible path forward?

**How to handle**: Pro includes 3 team seats. Agencies & Consultants tier handles multi-user, multi-client workspaces with unlimited seats. The jump in pricing is meaningful but not punishing relative to the revenue scale. Offer to put together a written proposal that models the cost at 40-person versus 80-person configuration so he can show the board both scenarios.

---

### Objection 4: No Contractual SLA
"If this platform is part of our BD infrastructure, I need an SLA. Uptime guarantees. Data freshness guarantees — if you're telling me 40,000 opportunities scored daily, I need to know what happens when SAM.gov goes down or you fall behind. What's contractual and what's a best-effort claim?"

**What he actually wants to know**: Is there accountability behind the product, or is this a startup that shrugs when things break?

**How to handle**: Be honest. Current pricing tiers are SaaS self-serve — no contractual SLA on Pro. Agencies & Consultants tier and the consulting engagement can include SLA language in a Master Services Agreement. If he needs an SLA today, the conversation needs to move to the consulting tier + custom contract. Frame this as a feature, not a gap: "The companies in your stage that we work with as consulting clients do get contractual commitments. If that's a requirement, let's talk about structuring this as a managed engagement rather than a self-serve subscription."

---

### Objection 5: References and Enterprise Credibility
"I need to talk to someone else who's using this at a comparable stage — $10M to $20M IT services firm, federal focus, going through a growth ramp. Can you give me two references?"

**What he actually wants to know**: Has this worked for anyone like me, or am I the case study?

**How to handle**: Do not fabricate references. If you have references, offer them immediately and be specific about the firm profile. If you are building the reference base, be transparent: "We're at a stage where our consulting clients are the strongest reference pool — I can connect you with two of them who are in similar situations. I would rather give you a real conversation than a staged reference call."

---

## Buying Signals

- Asks about volume discounts or multi-year pricing ("Is there a discount if we commit to two years?")
- Asks for a written proposal or a formal quote document ("Can you send me something I can take to the CEO?")
- Asks about implementation timeline and onboarding ("How long before we're actually getting usable output?")
- Asks what the consulting hours model looks like in practice ("If I buy the retainer, what does a typical month look like — what do you actually do?")
- Starts asking about the Agencies & Consultants tier even though he opened the conversation asking about Pro
- Asks what happens if the CEO wants to review the vendor ("Would you be available for a 30-minute call with our CEO?")

---

## Walk-Away Triggers

- You give him a ROI number you cannot back up with a model ("Customers typically see 10x ROI" with no framework behind it)
- You cannot name a single contractual commitment the platform makes
- You have no references that match his profile (IT services, $10M–$20M, federal growth mandate)
- You pressure him on timeline ("This offer is only good through end of month")
- You cannot answer the GovWin comparison with specifics — he will assume you have not done the homework
- You are vague about what the AI Proposal Writer actually produces ("Is this a first draft or is this a submittal-ready document? What's the hallucination risk on FAR compliance language?")

---

## Unstated Concerns

These are things Marcus is thinking but will not say out loud in the first conversation:

1. "If we miss the 3x, my position is at risk. I am not just buying BD software — I am buying cover."
2. "I'm not sure the part-time BD person can actually operate whatever I buy. I might need to hire someone, and that changes the total cost of this decision by $80K to $120K."
3. "The CEO is excited about AI. I am not. I've watched 'AI-powered' tools fail for three years. I need to see it work before I trust it."
4. "Is this company going to exist in 24 months? I need to understand the vendor's financial stability before I build infrastructure on top of it."

---

## Conversation Flow

### Cold Open (Marcus initiates or responds to outreach)

"Thanks for making time. I'll be direct — I've got about 30 minutes and I'm evaluating three vendors. I've already spoken with GovWin and I have a call with a consulting firm next week. So walk me through what you actually do and how you're different. Start with the platform."

**Coaching note**: Do not start with a feature tour. Start by naming his situation back to him: "$4M to $12M in 24 months, part-time BD, no structured pipeline. That's the problem we're solving. Here's how." Lead with the outcome, not the tool.

---

### After the Demo — The Spreadsheet Moment

After seeing the platform, Marcus will open his spreadsheet. He will say something like:

"Okay. So on Pro at $89/month, I'm at $1,068 for the year on software. If I add the consulting retainer you mentioned — what does that look like, and what do I get? Walk me through a month."

**Coaching note**: This is the close setup. Do not oversell the consulting. Be specific about what a typical month includes: kickoff call + profile setup in week one, curated opportunity shortlist + competitive briefs on 3–5 priority pursuits per month, one proposal draft per RFP response, monthly pipeline review call. Then anchor to the budget: "You said $80K for the year. At $2,400/month for the retainer plus $854 for Pro annual, you're at $29,654 for the year — 37% of your budget. That leaves $50K for internal BD headcount or other tools. You're not spending the whole budget on us, and you're getting the full stack: software plus the team."

---

### The Annual Prepay Ask

If Marcus has moved past the objections and is in spreadsheet mode, introduce the annual prepay discount:

"One thing worth modeling: if you prepay annually on the consulting engagement rather than month-to-month, we can give you a rate that saves you roughly $2,400 over the year. On a $80K budget, that's a meaningful line item."

**Coaching note**: Frame this as budget efficiency, not a close tactic. Marcus will see through close tactics. This is a real dollar number on a real budget line.

---

### The CEO Escalation Path

Marcus will likely end the first call with: "I need to take this to the CEO before I can commit. Can you send me a written summary?"

**Coaching note**: This is not a stall — this is the actual process. Offer to do a 30-minute executive briefing directly with the CEO + Marcus together, framed as "we can save you the back-and-forth translation layer." Send a one-page written summary within 24 hours. The summary should include: situation recap, proposed configuration, 12-month cost model, ROI framework (with inputs, not a fake number), and two reference contacts.

---

## Optimal Close Configuration

**Package**: Pro Annual ($854/yr) + Consulting Retainer ($2,400/mo, annual prepay at a discount)

**Annual total**: Approximately $29,654

**Budget headroom**: $50,346 remaining of the $80K — which you can note gives him room for the internal BD hire he is going to need anyway

**Anchor**: This is under the single-seat GovWin IQ price and includes a done-for-you capture team

**Commitment ask**: Annual prepay on the consulting retainer for the discount; Pro annual subscription for the platform

**Next step from first call**: 30-minute CEO + Marcus joint briefing, written proposal within 24 hours

## Sample Dialogue Fragments

**Marcus opening a price objection:**
"Just to make sure I understand — you're saying $89 a month gets me the full platform. GovWin was $32,000 a year. I'm not saying more expensive is better, but I need to understand what the price difference represents. Is this a different kind of product, or is it the same product at a different maturity level?"

**Marcus mid-demo, testing the AI:**
"Okay, walk me through what the AI Proposal Writer actually produces. Is this a first draft that my BD person has to rewrite, or is this a document I could submit? And how does it handle FAR compliance language — what's the error rate on that?"

**Marcus checking assumptions:**
"You mentioned 40,000 opportunities scored daily. Just to make sure I understand — is that 40,000 unique opportunities, or is that 40,000 scoring events, some of which are updates to the same opportunity? And when you say 'scored against my profile,' what does the profile consist of? NAICS codes, certifications — what else?"

**Marcus closing in on a decision:**
"What I need from you before I can make a decision: a written proposal with the configuration we discussed, a 12-month cost model, and two reference contacts I can call. Can you have that to me by end of week?"

## Trainer Notes

This persona is designed to train reps on financially literate buyers who control discretionary budgets and answer to a board. Key skills being tested:

1. **ROI framework construction** — Can the rep build a credible input-based model rather than citing an unverifiable claim?
2. **GovWin competitive handling** — Can the rep compare on the right dimensions (total cost of ownership, time-to-value, operational complexity) rather than just on price?
3. **Consultative pacing** — Can the rep follow Marcus's rhythm instead of rushing to close? He will disengage if pressured.
4. **Honesty on gaps** — Marcus respects honest acknowledgment of what the platform does not do more than a defensive deflection. The SLA objection is the test: the right answer is "we don't have a contractual SLA on Pro, but here's the path to one."
5. **Executive escalation readiness** — Can the rep offer the CEO joint briefing naturally, without making it feel like an upsell move?

**Difficulty level**: High. Marcus is polite, patient, and methodical — which means reps will feel the conversation is going well right up until he says "send me a proposal" and never responds again. The close requires proactive structure from the rep: specific next steps, specific deliverables, specific timeline.

**Scenario variations**:
- Marcus reveals at the end of the call that the CEO wants to expand to 80 people in 12 months, not 24 — compress the urgency
- Marcus mentions a competitor (by name, fictional) that quoted $5K one-time for a custom pipeline build — test the rep's response to a non-comparable alternative
- Marcus's board has just approved a $200K BD budget (double) and he is now evaluating whether to hire a full-time capture manager instead of buying software — forces the rep to reframe around build vs. buy
