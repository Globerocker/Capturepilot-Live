# 1 — The 10-step checklist before you bid

Most first-time federal bidders skip 4 of these 10 steps and lose. Here's the order:

1. **Get a UEI from SAM.gov** — Replaces the old DUNS number. Free. Takes 1-3 business days. Without it you literally cannot bid.
2. **Complete SAM.gov "active" registration** — Active status takes 7-10 days after UEI. Renew annually or you go inactive and contracts you've won can get terminated.
3. **Pick your NAICS codes** — 1 primary + up to 5 secondary. Wrong picks here can shut you out of 80% of relevant opportunities. (See Section 3.)
4. **Confirm your SBA size standard** — Tied to NAICS. For 541511 (custom programming) it's $34M revenue avg over 3 years. Different per NAICS.
5. **Decide on certifications** — 8(a), HUBZone, WOSB, SDVOSB, VOSB. Most are free. Some take 90 days. (See Section 5.)
6. **Write a Capability Statement** — Non-negotiable. One page. Government readers spend ~30 seconds on it. (See Section 4.)
7. **Identify 3 target agencies** — Don't say "all of government." Pick three. Research their forecasts, their incumbents, their preferred contract vehicles.
8. **Find 5 incumbents in your space** — These are your future teaming partners or competitors. Both useful.
9. **Find 5 Sources Sought notices in your NAICS** — Respond to all of them. (See Section 6.)
10. **Set a 12-month capture pipeline** — 8-12 active opportunities at any time. Track stage, deadline, win-probability.

If you can't tick all 10 in the next 60 days, you're not ready to bid. Fix the gaps first, then bid.

# 2 — SAM.gov registration walkthrough

SAM.gov (System for Award Management) is the front door. Every federal contractor must be registered.

## Step-by-step

1. **Go to** `sam.gov` and create a Login.gov account if you don't have one.
2. **Verify identity** with Login.gov — passport, driver's license, or in-person at a post office. Takes 10-30 minutes.
3. **Request a UEI (Unique Entity ID).** This is your federal ID number. Free, 1-3 business days.
4. **Complete the Entity Registration.** You'll need:
   - Legal business name + address (must match your IRS records exactly)
   - EIN (Tax ID)
   - DUNS number (only if you registered before April 2022 — otherwise skipped)
   - Bank routing + account info (for EFT payments)
   - NAICS codes
   - Past performance reference contacts (optional but recommended)
5. **Submit and wait.** Active status takes 7-10 days. You'll get an email when it flips.

## Common stuck-points

- **"Address mismatch"** — SAM does a DnB / IRS cross-check. If your legal address on file doesn't match exactly, the registration stalls. Fix the mismatch with IRS first, then come back.
- **Bank account rejected** — SAM uses pre-notification to validate routing/account. If the bank doesn't ACK in 7 days, the registration fails. Call your bank and tell them to expect it.
- **Annual renewal** — Set a calendar reminder for 60 days before your anniversary date. Lapsed registration = ineligible for award.

## Free help

- **PTAC / APEX Accelerator** — Free 1-on-1 SAM.gov registration help. Find your local office at `apexaccelerators.us`.
- **SBA Resource Partners** — SCORE mentors, SBDC counselors. Free.

# 3 — How to pick the right NAICS codes

The biggest mistake first-time bidders make: copying NAICS codes from competitors.

## The real way

Open the official NAICS lookup: `naics.com/search`

1. **Type what you actually deliver in plain English.** "Web development" or "janitorial services" or "IT consulting."
2. **Pick the most specific code that fits.** Six digits, not four. Specific NAICS codes win more contracts because matching is exact.
3. **Cross-check the SBA size standard.** Visit `sba.gov/document/support-table-size-standards` and look up your code. Make sure your business stays under the threshold (either revenue or employee count).
4. **Add 2-4 closely-related secondary codes.** Don't shotgun 15 codes — agencies notice and rate you down.

## Examples

| What you do | Primary NAICS | Size standard |
|---|---|---|
| Custom software development | 541511 | $34M revenue avg |
| IT consulting | 541512 | $34M revenue avg |
| Janitorial services | 561720 | $22M revenue avg |
| Commercial construction | 236220 | $45M revenue avg |
| Cybersecurity services | 541512 or 541519 | $34M revenue avg |
| Marketing / advertising | 541810 / 541613 | $30.5M revenue avg |
| Heavy hauling / logistics | 484230 | $34M revenue avg |

## Why this matters

Federal opportunity feeds (including CapturePilot) score-match opportunities to your NAICS. Wrong codes = wrong opportunities flooding your inbox. Right codes = 10× signal-to-noise.

# 4 — The 1-page Capability Statement

This is the single most-used document in federal sales. Government buyers read it in 30 seconds. Make every word count.

## Required sections (in order)

1. **Company name + logo + UEI/CAGE** — Top-left header.
2. **NAICS codes + set-aside certifications** — Top-right header.
3. **Core competencies** — 3-5 bullets, max. What you do better than anyone.
4. **Differentiators** — 3-5 bullets. WHY you, not just WHAT you do.
5. **Past performance** — 3-5 contracts with: customer name, contract # or vehicle, period, dollar value, scope. Anonymize if NDA-bound.
6. **Corporate data** — DUNS/UEI, CAGE, NAICS, certifications, year founded, # employees, bonding capacity (if relevant), states served.
7. **Point of contact** — Name, title, phone, email. Real human, not info@.

## What to AVOID

- ❌ Two-page sprawl — One page or it goes in the trash.
- ❌ Marketing fluff ("world-class", "innovative solutions", "synergistic")
- ❌ Stock photos of generic office workers
- ❌ Past performance with dollar values redacted to "$$$"
- ❌ Contact email like info@yourdomain.com

## Quick test

Email a draft to 5 federal small business specialists (SBSPs) at agencies you target. Ask: "Can you tell from this what we do in 30 seconds?" Their feedback is gold.

# 5 — Which certification should you actually pursue?

## The big 5 (most common)

| Certification | Best for | Time | Cost | Renewal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SBA Small Business** | Almost everyone under size threshold | Automatic via SAM.gov | $0 | Annual (SAM) |
| **8(a) Business Development** | Owned 51%+ by socially/economically disadvantaged individual | 90-120 days | $0 (DIY) or $5-15K consultant | 9-year program |
| **HUBZone** | Office + 35%+ of employees in HUBZone | 60-90 days | $0 | Annual recertification |
| **WOSB / EDWOSB** | 51%+ owned + controlled by women | 30-60 days | $0 (self-cert via certify.SBA.gov) | Every 3 years |
| **SDVOSB / VOSB** | 51%+ owned by service-disabled / veteran | 60-90 days via VetCert | $0 | Every 3 years |

## Quick decision tree

- **Veteran with service-connected disability?** SDVOSB first — biggest sole-source authority ($5M).
- **Female-majority-owned + work in male-dominated NAICS?** EDWOSB first (smaller competitor pool than WOSB).
- **Socially disadvantaged (race / ethnicity recognized by SBA) + economically disadvantaged?** 8(a) — life-changing program, $7B/year in sole-source set-asides.
- **Office in a designated HUBZone area + can hire from it?** HUBZone — instant 10% price preference + sole-source up to $5M.
- **None of the above?** Just SBA Small Business — still gets you in front of 23% of all federal spend.

## When certifications are NOT worth it

- You're already big enough to compete unrestricted in your NAICS.
- Your ownership/control structure won't survive the 51% test.
- You can't sustain the office/employee location requirement (HUBZone).
- You're chasing a single big contract that doesn't have the set-aside.

# 6 — Why Sources Sought is where you really start

Most first-time bidders see a Solicitation (RFP) and panic-write a proposal in 14 days. They lose.

The real game starts 6-18 MONTHS before the RFP, when agencies post a **Sources Sought** (also called RFI — Request for Information).

## What a Sources Sought actually is

A pre-solicitation post where the agency says: *"We're thinking about buying X. Who out there can do it? Tell us your capabilities."*

## Why responding matters

1. **You shape the requirement.** Your response can move the future RFP toward your strengths — narrower scope, specific NAICS, set-aside designation.
2. **You get on the shortlist.** Agencies use Sources Sought responses to decide WHO to invite for the real RFP — and whether to make it a set-aside at all.
3. **You learn the agency's pain.** The questions they ask reveal what they're worried about. You build your future proposal around those concerns.
4. **You start the relationship.** Following up with the SBSP after a Sources Sought is the #1 way to get a 1-on-1 capabilities briefing.

## How to respond

- **One page MAX.** They don't want a proposal — they want capabilities.
- **Use their question structure.** If they ask 4 questions, answer 4 questions.
- **Include past performance.** 2-3 relevant examples.
- **State your set-aside status clearly.** "We are an SDVOSB."
- **Submit before the deadline.** They count responses to decide whether to set-aside.

# 7 — First-bid checklist: 5 must-haves

Before you submit ANY proposal, verify ALL 5:

✅ **1. Compliance matrix.** Every requirement in Section L (instructions) and Section M (evaluation) of the RFP, mapped to where in your proposal you address it. Missing one item = automatic non-compliant = throw-away.

✅ **2. Reps & Certs current in SAM.** Every checkbox on your SAM.gov Reps & Certs page must be current. Auditors will check. A wrong answer here is grounds for award termination.

✅ **3. Past performance writeups for every relevant contract.** Customer name, contract #, period of performance, dollar value, technical scope, problems solved, results. Don't just list — narrate.

✅ **4. Pricing volume sealed and signed.** Pricing goes in a SEPARATE volume (Volume II usually). Signed by your authorized representative.

✅ **5. Submit via the prescribed method.** SAM.gov upload, email to specific address, hand-delivery, fax — whatever the RFP says. The WRONG channel = late submission = throw-away. Submit 24 hours early in case anything breaks.

## Bonus: the "Past performance gap" hack

No federal past performance yet? Use:

- Commercial contracts of similar scope (acceptable as past performance for most CO's).
- Subcontracts under a prime (count, even though you weren't direct).
- Government contracts at the state, county, or city level (less weight than federal, but counts).

# 8 — The 7 most common rejection reasons

After reviewing 500+ post-award debriefs, the same 7 mistakes repeat:

1. **Non-compliant proposal format.** Page limits exceeded, font too small, wrong section headers. Trivial to fix, fatal to ignore. **Read Section L word-for-word.**
2. **Missing required Reps & Certs.** Especially DFARS clauses for DoD bids. SAM page must match what the RFP requires.
3. **Past performance not relevant or too old.** Most CO's only count past performance from the last 5 years and only if technically similar.
4. **Price unrealistic (too low OR too high).** Too low = "doesn't understand the work, risky." Too high = "doesn't pencil out." Use FPDS to research what similar contracts have actually paid.
5. **No clear discriminator.** If your proposal could have been written by 20 other firms, it will lose to whichever of those 20 priced lower.
6. **Generic past performance writeups.** "We delivered the work on time and on budget" — meaningless. "We replaced 14 HVAC units in 22 occupied buildings over 6 weeks with zero tenant complaints" — winning.
7. **Submitted late or wrong channel.** Zero forgiveness. The clock is the clock.

Fix these 7 and you're already in the top 25%.

# 9 — Glossary: 25 acronyms you'll actually see

| Acronym | Means | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| UEI | Unique Entity ID | Your 12-char federal ID. Replaces DUNS. |
| CAGE | Commercial And Govt Entity | A 5-char code DoD uses to identify you. Free with SAM. |
| NAICS | North Am. Industry Classification System | 6-digit industry code. Tied to size standard. |
| PSC | Product / Service Code | Government's parallel taxonomy. Often listed alongside NAICS. |
| RFP | Request for Proposal | The big formal bid request. |
| RFQ | Request for Quote | Simpler, usually for goods or simple services. |
| RFI | Request for Information | Same as Sources Sought — pre-solicitation. |
| SSN | Sources Sought Notice | Pre-solicitation. Tell them you exist. |
| IFB | Invitation for Bid | Sealed-bid, lowest-price-wins. Rare these days. |
| GSA | General Services Admin | The agency that runs governmentwide contracts. |
| IDIQ | Indefinite Delivery / Indefinite Quantity | Pre-negotiated contract vehicle for ordering later. |
| GWAC | Governmentwide Acquisition Contract | IT-specific IDIQ usable across agencies. |
| BPA | Blanket Purchase Agreement | Open-end ordering vehicle off GSA Schedule. |
| MAS | Multiple Award Schedule | GSA Schedule — biggest single contract vehicle. |
| SAP | Simplified Acquisition Procedures | Contracts $10K-$250K. Easier rules. |
| MAP | Micro-Purchase Threshold | $10K-and-under, easiest contracts. Government credit card. |
| 8(a) | Section 8(a) BD Program | SBA's program for socially/economically disadvantaged. |
| HUBZone | Historically Underutilized Business Zone | Office + 35% of staff in designated area. |
| SDVOSB | Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned SB | 51%+ owned by service-disabled vet. |
| WOSB / EDWOSB | Women / Economically-Disadvantaged WOSB | 51%+ owned by women. |
| FAR | Federal Acquisition Regulation | The bible. Every clause starts with "FAR 52.xxx". |
| DFARS | Defense FAR Supplement | DoD-specific add-ons to FAR. |
| CO | Contracting Officer | The only person who can legally bind the government to a contract. |
| COTR / COR | Contracting Officer's Tech Rep | The CO's day-to-day delegate after award. |
| SBSP | Small Business Specialist | The agency person who shepherds SBs through procurement. |
| FPDS | Federal Procurement Data System | Free public database of every federal award since 1979. |
