# Loom scripts — Match-Drop outreach (Sergio)

Five short talking-head scripts, one per problem we detect on a contractor's
website. Each pairs with the **Match-Drop** email (we send the matches + findings
in writing; the Loom makes it personal).

## How to use these
- **Glance at the site first (10 seconds).** Only claim a gap you can actually
  see is missing. If their NAICS *is* on the site, use a different script. A
  wrong claim kills trust instantly.
- **Pull their real data** from the contractor record in the admin: the 3
  matches (`top_matches`) and the auto-detected `gap_hook` are already computed
  per contractor. Read the top match titles out loud — specificity sells.
- **Keep it 60–90 seconds.** Loom drop-off is brutal after 90s. One problem,
  three matches, one ask.
- **Talk like a person, not a pitch.** Slightly informal, a little weary-expert.
  You're doing them a favor, not selling.
- **Record once, reuse the structure.** The opener and close are the same; only
  the middle (the finding) changes.
- **Send the Loom in the email**, right under the 3 matches, with a line like
  "Made you a 60-second video walking through what we found."

Placeholders: `{first_name}`, `{company}`, `{match_1/2/3}`, `{state}`.

## Script 1 — NAICS codes not on the website
> The most universal one. Almost no small contractor lists their NAICS on their
> marketing site, so this is nearly always true — but still glance first.

Hey {first_name}, Sergio here from Americurial — quick one, 60 seconds.

I ran {company}'s site through our own software, the same checker we use for
federal contractors. First thing it flagged: your NAICS codes aren't anywhere on
your site. Sounds small, but here's why it matters — when a contracting officer
or a prime is sizing you up, that's one of the first things they look for. No
codes on the site, and they can't quickly tell what you're cleared to do, so a
lot of them just move on.

That was one of four findings. The bigger thing is, while it was running, it
pulled three live opportunities that actually fit {company} right now — I'll drop
those in the email under this video.

So my question is just: would you be open to getting back after government work,
especially if I told you there's basically no risk to it — we run the bids, you
only pay if you actually win one? If that's interesting, hit reply and I'll send
the full rundown. Either way, talk soon.

## Script 2 — No federal-readiness signal on the site
> Use when the site reads purely commercial — nothing about government, agencies,
> contracts, or past federal work.

Hey {first_name}, Sergio from Americurial — give me 60 seconds.

I put {company}'s site through our checker, and the thing that jumped out: there's
nothing on there that signals you do federal work. No mention of agencies, no
contract experience, nothing a government buyer would recognize. Now, I'm guessing
you *can* do the work — but a contracting officer doesn't know that from your
site, and that's the page they land on before they ever call you.

That was one of a few findings. The useful part: the same scan pulled three live
opportunities that line up with what you actually do — they're in the email right
below this.

I'd love to walk you through all of it. And look, the way we work is no-risk —
we find the bids, we prep them, you only pay if you win. Worth a quick chat to see
if it's worth getting back on the government track? Reply and I'll send everything
over.

## Script 3 — No past performance / client list shown
> Use when there's no project list, case studies, or "who we've worked with."

Hey {first_name}, it's Sergio at Americurial — real quick.

I ran {company} through our software and one thing stood out: there's no past
performance anywhere on your site. No client list, no projects, nothing a buyer
can point to and go "okay, these folks have done this before." In federal work
that's the whole game — past performance is what they trust. If it's not visible,
you're starting every conversation from zero.

That's one of four findings I'd love to walk you through. And the reason I'm
reaching out at all: the scan found three open opportunities that fit you right
now. They're in the email under this video.

Quick question — would it be worth getting back into government contracting if it
cost you nothing up front? Because that's how we run it: we do the bidding, you
only pay on a win. Hit reply if you want the full picture.

## Script 4 — Certifications / set-asides not visible
> Use when they likely hold (or qualify for) a cert but it's not on the site.

Hey {first_name}, Sergio here from Americurial — 60 seconds, promise.

I checked {company}'s site with our checker and noticed your certifications aren't
listed anywhere. Here's why that's a real miss: a huge chunk of federal work is
set aside specifically for certified small businesses — 8(a), HUBZone, veteran-
owned, women-owned. If a buyer can't see which lane you're in, they can't route
that easier work to you, and that's some of the least competitive money out there.

That was one of the findings. The other reason I'm reaching out — the scan pulled
three live opportunities that fit {company}. They're in the email below.

So, honest question: if there were no risk to it — we run the bids, you only pay
when you win — would you want back in on government work? Reply and I'll send you
everything we found.

## Script 5 — Unclear positioning / no capability statement
> Use when the site is vague about what they actually do in agency terms.

Hey {first_name}, Sergio from Americurial — quick note for you.

I ran {company}'s site through our software, and the thing it flagged is that it's
not clear, in the words an agency actually searches for, what you do. There's no
capability statement, nothing that translates your work into government language.
And that's the gap — a contracting officer searches in their terms, and if your
site doesn't speak that language, you just don't come up.

That's one of four findings. More to the point, the same scan found three live
opportunities that fit you right now — they're in the email right under this.

Here's my ask: would you be open to taking another run at federal work if it was
genuinely no-risk? We handle the bidding, you only pay if you win. If that's worth
15 minutes, reply and I'll send the whole breakdown over.

## Closing-line variants (swap to keep them fresh)
- "…would you be open to getting back on the government track? No risk — you only pay if you win."
- "…is this even on your radar this year? If it is, there's no downside to a look — we only get paid when you do."
- "…worth 15 minutes? Worst case you walk away with three live opportunities and a punch-list for your site."
- "…if I'm wrong and this isn't a fit, just say so and I'll leave you alone. But I think it's worth a conversation."

## Why this works
We lead with **value we already produced** (3 real matches) and **a concrete,
verifiable problem** (the gap) — not a generic pitch. That puts us in the helping
seat before we've asked for anything, and the no-risk / commission close removes
the only real objection. The Loom makes it land as a person, not a blast.
