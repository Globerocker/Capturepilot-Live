# Loom scripts — Match-Drop outreach (Sergio)

Five short talking-head scripts, **one per problem**, written to be **recorded
once and sent to everyone** in that segment — no names, no company placeholders.
The personalization (their actual 3 matches + findings) lives in the email; the
Loom's job is to **break the "here's another sales pitch" reflex** and get them to
not delete it.

Voice: a real person who's a little amused, slightly self-deprecating, and
clearly *not* a closer. Name the elephant ("yeah, this looks like every other
pitch"), then earn the next ten seconds. Dry humor, not goofy — these are
no-nonsense operators, often veterans.

## Priority — pick by how often we find each gap (over ~2,400 QC'd contractors)
| Gap | We see it on | Script |
|---|---|---|
| No NAICS codes on the site | ~95%+ (verify by glance) | **#1** |
| No past performance shown | **89%** | #2 |
| Certifications not visible | **85%** | #3 |
| No federal-experience signal | **81%** | #4 |
| No "years in business" shown | **72%** | #5 |

## How to use these
- **Record each once.** No names = send the same video to the whole segment.
- **Match the script to the segment** — the gap is already detected per contractor
  (`gap_hook` / `findings_summary` on the record), so send the right one.
- **The Loom is the hook, the email is the proof.** The 3 real matches + their
  specific findings go in the Match-Drop email under the video.
- **Don't read it word-for-word.** Hit the beats, sound like you. Look at the
  camera, not the script. A flubbed-but-human take beats a perfect robotic one.
- **45–75 seconds.** One problem, the "and we found matches" line, the no-risk ask.
- **NAICS (#1) is manual** — glance at the site for 10 seconds first so the claim
  is true. The other four are auto-detected.

## Script #1 — NAICS codes not on the website  *(manual — glance first)*

Okay — I already know what this looks like. Some guy you've never met pops up in
your inbox saying he can get you government contracts. You've seen forty of these.
So let me skip the part where I pretend we're friends.

Here's the only thing that makes this worth ten seconds: I'm not guessing about
you. We ran your actual site through our own software before I recorded this. And
the thing it flagged — you've got no NAICS codes anywhere on your site. I know,
sounds like nothing. But that's literally one of the first things a contracting
officer or a prime checks when they're deciding whether you're worth a call. Not
there? You're invisible to a big chunk of them before you've said a word.

That was one of a few things we found. We also pulled a handful of live contracts
that actually fit what you do — those are in the email under this.

And my whole "pitch" is embarrassingly simple: we do the bidding, you pay us
nothing unless you actually win. That's the deal. If that's even a little
interesting, just hit reply. If not — no hard feelings, delete away.

## Script #2 — No past performance / client list shown  *(89%)*

Real talk — you're probably half a second from deleting this, and honestly that's
the correct instinct for 99% of the videos like this. So here's why I'd give this
one the other half-second.

We didn't guess. We ran your site through our own software first, and here's what
stood out: there's no past performance on it anywhere. No client list, no
projects, nothing a buyer can point at and go "okay, these people have done this
before." And in federal work, that's the entire ballgame — they buy on trust, and
trust is past performance. If it's invisible, every conversation starts at zero.

That's one of the things we found. We also pulled a few live contracts that line
up with what you do — they're in the email below this video.

The offer's not complicated: we run the bids, and you pay us nothing unless you
win. So there's genuinely no downside to just looking. Reply if you want me to
send the rest. If not, all good.

## Script #3 — Certifications / set-asides not visible  *(85%)*

I'll be honest with you — I almost didn't record this, because I know exactly how
it lands. Stranger, webcam, "government contracts." Ugh. But we found something
specific enough that I figured it was worth it.

We ran your site through our software, and your certifications aren't on it
anywhere. Here's why that one actually stings: a massive chunk of federal work is
set aside *specifically* for certified small businesses — 8(a), HUBZone, veteran-
owned, women-owned. That's the least competitive money out there. But if a buyer
can't see which lane you're in, they can't hand it to you. You're leaving the easy
stuff on the table without knowing it.

That was one finding. We also found a few live contracts that fit you — they're in
the email.

And the way we work takes the risk off you completely: we do the bidding, you only
pay if you win. Worth a reply? If not, no worries at all.

## Script #4 — No federal-experience signal on the site  *(81%)*

Look, I'm gonna assume you get a dozen of these a week and your delete finger is

We ran your site through our own software before I hit record, and the thing it
flagged is this: there's nothing on it that tells a government buyer you do federal
work. No agencies, no contracts, none of the signals they look for. Now — I'm
guessing you actually *can* do the work. But a contracting officer doesn't know
that from your site, and that's the page they land on before they ever pick up the
phone. Right now it's quietly telling them "commercial only."

That's one of the things we found. We also pulled a few live contracts that fit
what you do — they're in the email under this.

Here's the whole offer: we find the work, we run the bids, you pay nothing unless
you win. No retainer, no risk. If that's worth a conversation, just reply.
Otherwise, genuinely, no hard feelings.

## Script #5 — No "years in business" / track record shown  *(72%)*

Alright, I know — a stranger's face just showed up in your inbox talking about
government contracts, and your instinct is to nuke it. Smart instinct. Give me ten
seconds anyway.

We ran your site through our software, and one thing jumped out: there's nothing
on it that says how long you've been around. No "founded in," no track record up
front. Feels minor — it's not. Every federal award is a risk decision, and "how
long have these folks been doing this" is one of the first gut-checks a buyer
makes. If your site doesn't answer it, you're making them guess, and people don't
bet on a guess.

That was one finding. We also found a few live contracts that actually fit you —
those are in the email.

And the offer's about as low-risk as it gets: we do the bidding, you pay only when
you win. If that's even mildly interesting, hit reply. If not, no hard feelings —
you can go back to deleting things.

## Closing-line swaps (keep them feeling unscripted)
- "…hit reply. If not, no hard feelings — delete away."
- "…just reply. Worst case you walk away with a few live contracts and a punch-list for your site."
- "…if I'm wrong and this isn't for you, tell me to get lost and I will. But I think it's worth a look."
- "…you pay us nothing unless you win, so the only thing a reply costs you is the reply."

## Why this works
Generic-but-specific: the video breaks the pattern (we name that it looks like a
pitch, then prove we actually looked at them), and the email carries the proof
(their real matches + findings). Leading with a problem we found + a no-risk
commission offer puts us in the helping seat, not the selling seat — which is the
only way a cold video gets a reply.
